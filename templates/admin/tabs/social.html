Social Notification Relay
Master kill-switch for forwarding Nostr notifications (mentions, replies, reposts, reactions — fediverse ones included) to Telegram (on by default). Each user still opts in under User Settings → Telegram; uncheck this only to disable the feature server-wide.
How often to check for new notifications (minimum 15). Restart after changing.
Fediverse server (ActivityPub)
Every local user with a name on this node (the NIP-05 names on the Relay tab) automatically becomes @name@your domain — Mastodon, Pleroma, Misskey and friends can find, follow and reply to them. Nothing is kept in a separate database: posts that arrive are stored as Nostr events (the same puppet accounts the bridge below uses), and members' own Nostr posts are sent out as they are. A member who already reaches the fediverse through their own linked Pleroma account keeps doing so and is not sent twice. Instances are blocked on the Relay tab (Blocked bridges/relays) — that one list covers this too. Takes effect without a restart.
Not only local users: anyone on Nostr this relay knows is @npub1…@your domain. Fediverse people can follow, mention and reply to them (delivered to their own relays), and their replies, mentions, likes and boosts of fediverse posts go out.
Fediverse direct messages arrive as encrypted Nostr DMs, and Nostr DMs to fediverse accounts are delivered to them. This server reads these messages as it carries them — that is what a bridge between two encryption systems is. A fediverse account can only message someone who follows it or has written to it first.
Where /.well-known/webfinger answers. Leave blank to use the NIP-05 domain, so name@domain on Nostr and @name@domain on the fediverse are one identity. Changing it later breaks existing follows.
Servers whose public name resolves to a private address from here (split DNS — hosted behind the same router). They are otherwise refused, because this server never fetches private addresses for strangers. The Pleroma bridge's instance is trusted without being listed.
